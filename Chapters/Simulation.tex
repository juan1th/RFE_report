\subsubsection{Formulas and Calculations}
To determine the tuning sensitivity of the oscillator, the following formula is used:

\begin{equation}
    K_v = \frac{\Delta f}{\Delta V} = \frac{f_{\max} - f_{\min}}{V_{\max} - V_{\min}}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Formulas and Calculations}
To determine if a spurious emission is a harmonic, its frequency must be an integer multiple of the fundamental frequency ($f_1$):

\begin{equation}
    f_n = n \cdot f_1
\end{equation}

where $n$ is an integer ($2, 3, 4, \dots$).

\subsubsection{Formulas and Calculations}
For a standard RF mixer, the output frequency products ($f_{\text{RF}}$) are mathematically defined by the sum and difference of the Local Oscillator ($f_{\text{LO}}$) and Intermediate Frequency ($f_{\text{IF}}$) input signals:

\begin{equation}
    f_{\text{RF}} = |f_{\text{LO}} \pm f_{\text{IF}}|
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Formulas and Calculations}
The measurements rely on the fundamental relationship between the load impedance ($Z$) and the characteristic impedance of the system ($Z_w$, which is $50\,\Omega$ in this RF system). The reflection coefficient ($\rho$) at the load is defined as:

\begin{equation}
    \rho = \frac{Z - Z_w}{Z + Z_w}
\end{equation}

The Vector Network Analyzer displays these results on a Smith chart, which uniquely maps the complex normalized impedance plane ($z = Z / Z_w = r + jx$) to the complex reflection coefficient ($\rho$) plane.

\subsubsection{Short discussion of measurement results}

The measurements clearly illustrate the critical importance of proper VNA calibration and the behavior of parallel RF loads. Following calibration, the VNA accurately recorded the $75\,\Omega$ termination as $74.3\,\Omega$ with a negligible reactive component of $j0.3\,\Omega$, mapping correctly to the Smith chart's real axis. Similarly, the short circuit calibration was successful, yielding a near-ideal $-0.3\,\Omega$.

The experiment exploring makeshift terminations using the BNC Coax-Tee/Power Divider yielded excellent practical results. The ``One by 2'' termination measured approximately $50.2\,\Omega$, confirming a single standard load. When the ``Two by 2'' termination was measured, the impedance dropped to $24.8\,\Omega + j1.8\,\Omega$. This perfectly demonstrates the parallel resistance formula:

\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{total}} = \frac{R_1 \times R_2}{R_1 + R_2}
\end{equation}

where two $50\,\Omega$ loads connected in parallel via the tee result in an equivalent impedance of $25\,\Omega$.

Finally, Figure 2.5 highlights the impedance transformation effect of coaxial cables. When a short circuit is measured at the end of a transmission line without shifting the calibration plane to the end of the cable, the line acts as an impedance transformer, shifting the perceived impedance from $0\,\Omega$ to a highly complex value ($1.3\,\text{k}\Omega - j1.1\,\text{k}\Omega$) depending on the electrical length of the cable and the phase constant ($\beta$) at $48.923$ MHz.

\subsubsection{Formulas and Calculations}
The VNA measures the S-parameters directly in decibels (dB), representing the logarithmic ratio of the reflected or transmitted wave to the incident wave.

\begin{equation}
    \text{Return Loss (dB)} = -20 \log_{10} |\rho| = -20 \log_{10} |S_{11}| \quad \text{or} \quad -20 \log_{10} |S_{22}|
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \text{Transmission Loss (dB)} = -20 \log_{10} |S_{21}| \quad \text{or} \quad -20 \log_{10} |S_{12}|
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Short discussion of measurement results}

The measurements successfully document the return and transmission losses of the radio link. However, instead of the targeted $433$ MHz ISM band, the antennas tested showed primary resonances at different frequencies.

For the dual antenna setup, a usable transmission loss ($S_{21}$) of $-27.53$ dB was recorded at $972.33$ MHz, alongside a good return loss ($S_{22}$) of $-26.55$ dB at $970.92$ MHz. At $989.51$ MHz, the transmission loss drastically worsened to $-72.27$ dB, indicating very poor signal transfer at that specific frequency. The single antenna setup demonstrated an excellent return loss ($S_{11}$) of $-33.26$ dB at $260.13$ MHz, meaning very little power was reflected back to the source at this frequency.
